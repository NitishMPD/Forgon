# City Design
Author: Nitish

The city hierarchy presents a few readable categories first, with spatial building blocks, individual boulevards and assembled trees beneath them. Empty identity parents organize the scene without moving or resizing its contents. The user's standalone Box stays independent.

A miniature city on a square island, viewed from an elevated perspective. Four asphalt boulevards divide eight building blocks around a central green park. Warm stone, muted terracotta, teal, and slate facades alternate beneath pale roof cornices. Dark blue window bands articulate the floors.

Cream zebra crossings and pale yellow lane markings define the street grid. Trees, colorful parked cars, and a turquoise fountain add scale and landmarks. Taller buildings along the far edge form the skyline. A light blue sky, golden afternoon sun, and cool sky fill keep the scene bright with warm highlights and readable shaded facades. A restrained turquoise light accents the central fountain. Only the sun casts shadows to keep the lighting clear and inexpensive.

Geometry stays as ordinary editable entities; building details and car parts are parented to their owners. Existing editor selection and keyboard controls remain available. The expanded island preserves the street grid and landmark layout, with full-size parked cars and low pale stone walls defining all four city edges. The elevated camera frames the larger island.

During Play, the terracotta tour car loops around the park with rounded turns. Enter moves the view behind and above that car for third-person driving; the camera follows the car's heading and pulls closer when obstructed. A compact high-contrast controls hint sits at the bottom of the viewport. WASD and arrow keys provide equivalent controls, Space brakes, and R returns to the overview tour. Camera changes happen only in Play; stopping restores normal editing.

The enlarged city uses 120 individual buildings across eight dense blocks, with the central park retained. Buildings keep their original footprints rather than stretching the same 24 silhouettes. New buildings use simplified facade/cornice/window bands to keep the document bounded and editable.
